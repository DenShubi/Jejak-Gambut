How it works

NISAR GCOV HV and HH (pre and post, same track and frame) are speckle-filtered in linear power, then converted to dB. Inside mapped peat units only, a 3-channel change tensor (−ΔHV, ΔHH, −ΔHV/HH) is segmented into SLIC superpixels; a Multi-Otsu threshold on superpixel ΔHV separates canopy loss from stable forest. Each scar is then labelled: confirmed — a nominal/high VIIRS hotspot within 375 m; hidden — no hotspot on the scar, HV dropped while HH held (HV/HH ratio fell > 1 dB), organic shape (rectangularity < 0.78), and fire evidence nearby: a VIIRS fire within 3 km or dense smoke (AOD ≥ 1); verify — everything else: box-shaped (excavator clearing), ratio unchanged (e.g. flooding), or canopy loss with no fire nearby and clean air (likely plantation harvest). Spread direction runs from the centroid of the earliest-day hotspots to the scar centroid; for smoke-hidden scars it is the scar's long axis (±180°).

Limits. Peat only, so totals are not comparable with national burned-area figures. “Hidden” scars are radar evidence, not proven fires. AOD is MERRA-2 reanalysis (≈50 km). No deep learning: VIIRS labels would make validation circular.
